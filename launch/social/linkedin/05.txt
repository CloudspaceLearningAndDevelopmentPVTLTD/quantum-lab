Every theory in which the world has definite properties before you look is bound by one number: 2.

That is the CHSH inequality. Take four correlation measurements on a pair of particles, combine them as S = |E₁ − E₂ + E₃ + E₄|, and any local hidden-variable account of the universe — however clever, however contrived — gives you S ≤ 2. It is not a statement about apparatus. It is arithmetic about what "the properties were already there" can possibly produce.

Quantum mechanics gives 2.828.

The animation sweeps Bob's analyser while Alice holds at 0° and 45°, and the correlation climbs through the classical ceiling at 22.5°, touches 2√2, and falls away again. Nothing is sampled: E(a,b) = −cos 2(a−b) for the singlet, and the four terms at the peak are exactly ∓0.707.

Two things worth sitting with. The violation is not marginal — it is 41% past the bound, which is why the experiments were decisive once the loopholes closed. And quantum mechanics does not run away to 4, the algebraic maximum. It stops at 2√2, the Tsirelson bound, and nobody has a fully satisfying story for why the universe is exactly this non-local and no more.
